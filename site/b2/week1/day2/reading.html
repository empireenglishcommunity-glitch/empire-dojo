The Myth of the Finished Project

أسطورة المشروع اللي خلص

مقال بيقول إن الشغل المستمر أهم من الإنجاز اللي بيتعلن.

📄 Read / اقرا

We have been measuring the wrong thing.

Organisations celebrate the achievement they can announce, not the ongoing effort that produced it, and the result is a culture that rewards endings.

Consider a team that has been working on the same system for two years.

Lately they have improved reliability by a third, but there is nothing to launch, so their progress is invisible.

Meanwhile a second team accomplishes a small, temporary fix, announces it before the deadline, and receives the credit.

So far, so familiar.

My own view is that this is not merely unfair; it is expensive.

When people learn that only completed targets are counted, they stop focusing on the work that cannot be finished — maintenance, documentation, the slow development of skill.

Those are precisely the activities that decide whether an organisation still functions in five years.

Some managers reply that you cannot reward what you cannot measure.

That is true, but it is an argument for better measurement, not for none.

Progress can be described even when it cannot be photographed.

Until we learn to say 'this has been improving' as confidently as we say 'this is done', we will keep paying for the difference.

the difference.
