📄 Transcript / النص

Now read while you listen again — check what you missed. / اقرا وإنت بتسمع تاني — شوف إيه اللي فاتك.

Narrator
For eleven years the case was closed. It was reopened in twenty-nineteen, not because new evidence appeared, but because someone re-read the old evidence and noticed what it could not mean. Here is the reasoning, and I want you to follow it rather than accept it. The gate was locked from the inside. So whoever left must have had a key, or must have climbed the eastern wall. The wall was wet that night and unmarked in the morning, so they cannot have climbed it. Therefore they must have had a key. Eleven people had keys. But the original investigation had reached a different conclusion. It decided the gate might have been locked afterwards, by a caretaker who could simply have forgotten doing it. That is not impossible. Caretakers do forget. And once you allow that, the key stops mattering and the eleven names dissolve. This is the part that should trouble you. Both lines of reasoning are valid. One assumes the physical evidence is reliable and human memory is not; the other assumes the opposite. Neither can be tested now. What decided the case in nineteen ninety-eight was not evidence. It was which of those two assumptions the investigating officer found more natural, and he had been a caretaker himself, twenty years earlier, in a building with a gate he had sometimes forgotten to lock. He may never have noticed that about his own reasoning. Most of us don't.
