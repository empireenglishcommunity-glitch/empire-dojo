The Founder Nobody Names

المؤسِّسة اللي محدش بيسمّيها

مقال عن ناس مؤسّسين بيتنسوا من التاريخ الرسمي.

📄 Read / اقرا

The library in our district, which was built in 1931, carries the name of a governor who never entered it.

The woman who founded it, having sold her own house to pay for the land, is recorded nowhere on the building.

This is not unusual.

Landmarks tend to be named after the official who approved them rather than the resident who wanted them.

The monument in the square, surrounded now by parked cars, commemorates a battle; the school behind it, which has taught four generations of the same families, commemorates nobody.

Heritage bodies, whose job is partly to decide what is worth keeping, are aware of the problem.

A colleague of mine, a historian working in the region, has spent years collecting the names of founders known only from receipts and letters.

Her archive, containing over three hundred names, is not yet published.

What is at stake is not fairness to the dead.

It is the belief, held quietly by residents in every district, that places are made by people like them.

A city that names only its governors teaches its children that nothing they build will be remembered.
