The Register You Did Not Choose

النبرة اللي مااخترتهاش

مقال عن إن نبرة الكلام بتوصل رسالة قبل الكلمات.

📄 Read / اقرا

Every message carries two messages: what you said, and how close you assumed you were to the reader.

Most misunderstandings I have had to sort out at work came from the second one.

A colleague writes 'can you look into this?'

and means it warmly.

Read by somebody who was expecting 'would you mind looking into this?'

, it arrives as an instruction.

Neither person has done anything wrong; they have simply set up different assumptions about distance.

When we then try to deal with the misunderstanding, we usually discuss the task, which was never the problem.

This is why I would turn down the advice to 'just be natural' in a second language.

Natural in which relationship?

A learner who has picked up phrasal verbs from films may come up with something perfectly fluent and far too informal for the email they are writing.

The skill worth taking on is not formality.

It is range: being able to say the same thing three ways and to notice which one the situation is asking for.

Pay attention to that, and you will call off far fewer arguments you never meant to start.

meant to start.

❓ Question / سؤال 2

Why is 'can you look into this?' sometimes misread?

It is too long
It contains an informal phrasal verb that reads as rude
