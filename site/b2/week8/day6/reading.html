What the Interview Left Out

اللي المقابلة سكتت عنه

تحليل لمقابلة وإزاي طريقة النقل بتغيّر المعنى.

📄 Read / اقرا

A minister was interviewed on Tuesday, and by Wednesday two newspapers had reported the same twenty minutes very differently.

The first reported that she had admitted the programme was behind schedule and had refused to give a new date.

The second reported that she had explained the delay, pointed out that two suppliers had failed, and promised a revised timetable within a month.

Both accounts are defensible.

Neither is complete.

The difference lies in the reporting verbs.

'Admitted' invites the reader to assume guilt; 'explained' invites them to assume reason.

When a journalist writes that a politician denied something rather than that she disputed it, the sentence has already reached a verdict.

I would advise readers to do one small thing: notice the verb.

If an article tells you that somebody insisted, threatened or claimed, ask what a neutral account would have said instead.

Sometimes the answer is nothing — the verb was accurate.

Often the answer reveals that the reporting, not the speaker, supplied the emotion.

d the emotion.
