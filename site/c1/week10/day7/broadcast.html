📄 Transcript / النص

Now read while you listen again — check what you missed. / اقرا وإنت بتسمع تاني — شوف إيه اللي فاتك.

Statistician
So the index. Nine indicators, and the first thing to say — actually no, let me do this the other way round. Air quality has a threshold. There's a level above which harm is documented, we know where it is, it's a real number. Institutional trust does not have one. Nobody has ever specified the point at which trust becomes insufficient, because there isn't one. And we average them. We normalise both to one-to-ten and we average them, and the moment you do that you've asserted that a point of trust and a point of air quality are the same size, which — they're not, they're not even the same kind of thing. The extent to which people rely on the composite is what worries me. A city moves four places, and the press writes it up, and when you go and look, the movement is in three indicators none of which is individually significant. So it's precise. It's precise to two decimal places and it isn't accurate, and those get confused constantly. Where it does work — and it does work — is the wide comparison. If two cities are a long way apart, further apart than the error, the ranking holds, and we've validated that four times. Fine. Use it for that. The bands are quantiles, by the way, which means a difference of nought point two can move you a category, and the appendix says so, and I have never met anyone who read the appendix. And the last thing, which is really the first thing. If somebody asks you what a city's score means, the honest answer is that it means where that city sits relative to other cities on nine things we chose. Not how good it is. Those are not the same sentence and only one of them is defensible.
