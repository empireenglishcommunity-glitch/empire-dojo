🎯 The main point / الفكرة الأساسية

Answer this from listening. The transcript unlocks afterwards. / جاوب من السمع. النص بيتفتح بعد كده.

What does he say the real damage was?

قال إن الضرر الحقيقي كان إيه؟

The campaign exaggerated a need that was actually small
The six per cent figure was inaccurate
Unverifiable language handed opponents the argument that the campaign would not say how large the need was

❓ Question / سؤال 1

How many numbers were in the leaflet?

كام رقم كان في المنشور؟

None at all
One, in a footnote
Several, in the main text

❓ Question / سؤال 2

Why does he now prefer 'worse by six per cent'?

ليه بيفضّل دلوقتي 'أسوأ بستة في المية'؟

It is more emotionally persuasive
It is shorter to print
It survives contact with a hostile reader because it can be checked

❓ Question / سؤال 3

What is his attitude to his own earlier writing?

إيه موقفه من كتابته السابقة؟

He was being cynical at the time
He meant every word and still thinks the case is sound
He blames a colleague for it

📄 Transcript / النص

Now read while you listen again — check what you missed. / اقرا وإنت بتسمع تاني — شوف إيه اللي فاتك.

Campaign lead
I wrote most of it, so this is not me having a go at somebody else. Utterly urgent. Nowhere near adequate. By far the most effective option available. I wrote those. And I did mean them, that's the thing, I wasn't being cynical, I was absolutely convinced and I still am. But go and count the numbers in that leaflet. There's one. It's in a footnote. Provision fell six per cent over four years, and six per cent is real, it's more than enough on its own, and I buried it under 'vastly' and 'profoundly' and — what else — 'sheer scale', I think I wrote 'the sheer scale of the crisis'. Which cannot be checked. That's the point I've come round to. 'Considerably worse' can't be checked and 'worse by six per cent' can, and the second one is markedly less exciting, which is exactly why it's the one that survives contact with somebody hostile. We handed them the only argument they ever needed. Not that the need is small — nobody can say that, the need isn't small — but that the people describing it wouldn't say how large. And you can't come back from that in a follow-up, because now you're arguing about your own adjectives. Anyway. Next one, we lead with the six per cent. I've had that argument twice already and I expect to have it again on Thursday. What I'd say to anybody doing this work is: write the number first. Write the number, and then see whether you still need the adjective. Nine times out of ten you don't. And the tenth time you discover the number isn't there, which is worth finding out yourself, before somebody hostile finds it out for you in public.
