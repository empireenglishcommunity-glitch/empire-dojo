The Sheer Scale of It

الحجم الهائل

نص بيستخدم مبالغات كتير: الكاتب بيحلّل إزاي التشديد بيقنع من غير أدلة.

📄 Read / اقرا

The campaign material is remarkably consistent.

The need is "utterly urgent", the current provision "nowhere near adequate", the proposal "by far the most effective option available".

I do appreciate the sincerity behind this.

I also notice that not one of those three phrases contains a number.

Intensification is not deception, and it would be downright unfair to suggest otherwise.

Where a writer is thoroughly familiar with a subject and profoundly convinced, emphatic language is the natural register, and prose that refuses it altogether reads as evasive.

Far too much professional writing is flattened by a fear of seeming committed.

But emphasis substitutes for evidence with unusual ease, and it does so all the more effectively because it is not a claim.

"Considerably worse" cannot be checked.

"Worse by eleven per cent" can, and is markedly less persuasive, which is decidedly the point.

A reader who asks what "vastly" means is treated as pedantic, and a writer who avoids the word is thought weak.

The incentives run one way.

What did happen — and this is the part the material does state plainly, once, in a footnote — is that provision fell by six per cent over four years.

That is a real decline.

It is distinctly less than the text implies, and it is beyond doubt enough to justify the proposal on its own.

Which is what makes the rhetoric self-defeating.

The case is sound.

By underscoring it with language that cannot be verified, the campaign has made a verifiable argument look like an unverifiable one, and has invited the only rebuttal that was ever available: not that the need is small, but that the people describing it were unwilling to say how large.

It is usually inaccurate, since such precise-looking figures are rarely reliable in practice
