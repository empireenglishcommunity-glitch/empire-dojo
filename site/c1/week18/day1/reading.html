Or Rather, Let Me Put It Another Way

أو بالأحرى، خليني أقولها بطريقة تانية

نص عن الطلاقة: التصحيح الذاتي والاستطراد علامات كفاءة، مش ضعف.

📄 Read / اقرا

There is a persistent belief among advanced learners that fluency means never stopping.

The point I'm making is close to the opposite, and it is worth elaborating rather than asserting.

Consider what a proficient speaker actually does.

They signpost — "to return to your first question" — which allows a listener to survive a digression of thirty seconds without losing the thread.

They correct themselves openly: "the policy was introduced in 2011, or rather it was drafted in 2011 and introduced two years later."

And having said that, they recap at intervals, not because they have forgotten but because they can hear that the listener might have.

None of this is smooth.

Transcribed, it looks repetitive.

What it is, is coherent, and coherence is what the descriptors reward.

Spontaneity without signposting produces speech that is easy to produce and hard to follow, which is the failure mode of the confident learner.

Long-winded is not the opposite of succinct; both can be perfectly coherent, and both can fail.

To sum up — and in a nutshell this is the whole argument — the ability to hold the floor is not the ability to keep talking.

It is the ability to keep a listener oriented while you think.

Paraphrase, clarify, digress and consolidate: these are load-bearing, and a speaker who has none of them is not fluent but merely quick.

What I mean is this, and bear with me because it sounds like a paradox.

The most eloquent people you know are the ones you have most often heard say "let me put it another way".

They are not struggling.

They have simply noticed something you did not say, and they are the only kind of speaker who ever does.
