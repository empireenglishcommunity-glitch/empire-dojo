📄 Transcript / النص

Now read while you listen again — check what you missed. / اقرا وإنت بتسمع تاني — شوف إيه اللي فاتك.

Committee member
People keep telling me it was the price and it wasn't the price. Meridian were cheaper, yes, marginally, and the quality assessments came back equivalent, so on paper — I mean on paper you'd take Meridian. What I remember is Hala at the end of the table, and she'd been quiet for most of it, and she said something like, week fourteen against a week eleven deadline isn't a discount, it's a bill you haven't opened yet. Which stuck. It was then that the whole thing turned, I think, though nobody wrote it down that way. And there was the penalty clause. Castell agreed to one and Meridian wouldn't, and at the time that felt like a small procedural detail — it's the sort of thing you note and move past. Looking at it now it was the tell. A supplier who won't sign a penalty clause is telling you what they think their own chances are. Anyway, the minutes say 'delivery was decisive', which is true and tells you nothing, because delivery was decisive in a very specific way that took Hala one sentence to see and the rest of us forty minutes. What actually mattered, and I'd say this to anybody, is that the cheap option was cheap on the number we were looking at and expensive on the number we weren't. One more thing, since we're on it. I went back through my own notebook from that week and I'd written 'Meridian — cheaper?' with a question mark next to it, which tells you I half-saw it and didn't push. That's the part I'd change. Not the outcome. The outcome was right. The forty minutes it took us, when one person in the room had it in a sentence.
