The Rise and the Reading

الارتفاع وقراءته

نص أكاديمي مكثّف: الارتفاع في التشخيص مش نفس الارتفاع في الحالة.

📄 Read / اقرا

The rise in recorded diagnoses is not in dispute.

The interpretation of that rise is, and the distinction turns almost entirely on the reliability of detection rather than on any change in prevalence.

Three processes coincided after 2014.

The introduction of routine screening in secondary schools produced a step change in ascertainment.

The implementation of a broader diagnostic threshold widened the category itself.

And the expansion of regional clinics reduced the geographical scarcity of assessment, with the predictable consequence that areas previously showing low rates showed rates close to the national mean within two years.

The significance of this ordering is easily missed.

Because the three overlapped, the accumulation of cases looks like a single emergent trend.

Differentiation between them requires the raw distribution, which is available, and the correlation with clinic openings, which is strong enough that its omission from the 2019 summary is difficult to explain.

None of this supports the more comfortable conclusion, namely that nothing has changed.

Even after adjustment for ascertainment, a residual increase of roughly four per cent per decade persists, and its verification is the one genuinely open question.

The utilisation of services has risen far faster, which is a separate matter, and the deterioration in waiting times follows from that rather than from any change in the underlying condition.

The tendency in public discussion is to treat the whole rise as either artefact or emergency.

The compilation of evidence supports neither.

It supports a modest real increase, a large measurement effect, and an intervention problem in capacity that the diagnostic argument has been extremely useful in obscuring.
