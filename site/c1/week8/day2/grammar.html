Hedging and cautious language

التخفيف واللغة المتحفّظة

🔎 In short / بإيجاز

Soften and qualify claims: tend to, appear/seem to, it would seem, arguably, to some extent, by and large.

تخفيف وتقييد الادّعاءات: tend to، appear to، it would seem، arguably، to some extent.

🧩 The Pattern / النمط

tend to + verb · appear/seem to + verb · it would seem/appear that · arguably, presumably, admittedly · to some extent, by and large, on the whole, up to a point · may/might + verb

[Prices] [tend to] [rise] · [It would seem] [that nobody checked]

⚡ tend TO / appear TO / seem TO + base verb. Keep 'that' after 'it would seem'. Stacking two hedges is normal in academic English.

tend TO / appear TO / seem TO + المصدر. وأبقِ 'that' بعد 'it would seem'. وتجميع أداتي تخفيف أمر معتاد أكاديميًا.

📍 When to use it / امتى تستخدمه

To state a view while leaving room for disagreement — the default register of academic argument, professional disagreement and diplomatic writing. 'tend to' turns a claim into a tendency; 'appear/seem to' distances you slightly; 'arguably' concedes the point is debatable; 'to some extent' limits the scope. Hedging signals intellectual care, and its absence can read as arrogance.

لإبداء رأي مع إتاحة مجال للاختلاف — الأسلوب الافتراضي للنقاش الأكاديمي والخلاف المهني والكتابة الدبلوماسية. التخفيف يدل على دقّة فكرية، وغيابه قد يبدو تعجرفًا.

⚠️ Why this is tricky for Arabic speakers / ليه دي صعبة على العربي

Arabic hedges lexically (يبدو أنّ / إلى حدٍّ ما) but does not require the same density of qualification, so direct translation of an Arabic argument into English can sound unusually assertive to an academic reader. Structurally, learners omit 'to' after 'tend' and 'seem', and often stack hedges incorrectly. The deeper issue is calibration: knowing how much hedging a context expects is itself part of the C1 skill.

💡 Examples / أمثلة

Prices tend to rise during the winter months.

تميل الأسعار إلى الارتفاع في أشهر الشتاء

[tend to + base verb]

The data appears to suggest a different pattern.

تبدو البيانات مشيرة إلى نمط مختلف

[appears to + base verb]

It would seem that nobody checked the figures.

يبدو أن أحدًا لم يراجع الأرقام

[It would seem] + [that + clause]

Arguably, it was the right decision at the time.

يمكن القول إنه كان القرار الصحيح حينها

[Arguably], + clause

To some extent, that is a fair criticism.

إلى حدٍّ ما، هذا نقد عادل

[To some extent], + clause

🚫 Common mistakes / أخطاء شائعة

❌ Prices tend rise in winter.

✅ Prices tend to rise in winter.

'Tend' always takes 'to' before the verb.

❌ The data seems suggesting a pattern.

✅ The data seems to suggest a pattern.

'Seem' is followed by 'to' plus the base verb, not by an -ing form.

❌ It would seem nobody checked.

✅ It would seem that nobody checked.

In formal writing keep 'that' after 'it would seem'.

❌ In some extent, that is fair.

✅ To some extent, that is fair.

The fixed phrase uses 'to', not 'in'.
