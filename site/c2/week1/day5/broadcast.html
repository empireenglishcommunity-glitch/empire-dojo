🎯 The main point / الفكرة الأساسية

What do the two panellists turn out to agree about?

That the problem lies in the report's choice of adjective rather than in its figures
That the report's underlying figures are wrong and must be recalculated before publication
That the four staff who spoke to the press behaved improperly
That the committee should be replaced before the next review

❓ Question / سؤال 1

Why does Ms Haddad object to the phrase 'four people'?

Because she believes no member of staff was harmed at all
Because nineteen were on the rota and only four went to the press, so the phrase describes the wrong set
Because the audit names five people rather than four
Because Dr Aziz was not himself on the rota

❓ Question / سؤال 2

What does Dr Aziz concede about his own conduct?

That he should have written to the committee in March, and his reason for not doing so was itself self-serving
That he leaked the rota to the press in March
That he had not read the audit before speaking in print
That his published figures were taken from an early draft

❓ Question / سؤال 3

What distinction is Ms Haddad drawing with 'a claim and a convenience'?

Between asserting something yourself and letting a useful misreading stand uncorrected
Between a formal complaint and an informal one
Between what the audit says and what the department says
Between a claim spoken aloud in the meeting and one written down in the formal report afterwards

📄 Transcript / النص

Now read while you listen again — check what you missed. / اقرا وإنت بتسمع تاني — شوف إيه اللي فاتك.

Chair
Right, we've got eleven minutes, so I'm going to be rude and cut in. Dr Aziz — the department says the programme was resilient. You've said in print that resilience is doing a great deal of work in that sentence. Go.
Dr Aziz
It's doing all of the work. Look, nobody disputes the delivery figures. The figures are the figures. What I dispute is—
Ms Haddad
—the framing, yes, we've heard, but you can't keep—
Dr Aziz
Let me finish the sentence, Layla, and I'll hand it straight back. What I dispute is that a programme which ran eleven weeks over, and absorbed that overrun by asking its staff to absorb it, should be described with a word implying the system took the strain. The system did not take the strain. Four people did.
Chair
Ms Haddad.
Ms Haddad
And I'd accept every word of that except the last two. Because 'four people' is the bit that gets quoted, and it is the bit that is not in the audit. There were nineteen people on that rota. Four of them went to the press. Those are not the same set, and Fadi knows they aren't.
Dr Aziz
I have never claimed—
Ms Haddad
You've never claimed it. No. You have also never corrected anybody who read it that way, and there is a difference between a claim and a convenience.
Chair
That's — sorry, that is quite a serious charge, do you want to—
Dr Aziz
No. It's a fair charge and I'll take it. I should have written to the committee in March and I didn't, and my reason for not doing it was that I thought it would look self-serving, which is itself a self-serving reason. Fine. Noted.
Ms Haddad
...Well. That's a good deal more than I came expecting.
Chair
Thirty seconds each, then I have to stop us. What word should the report have used?
Dr Aziz
'Foreseeable'. Not about the port. About the rota.
Ms Haddad
I'd have used no adjective whatsoever. Eleven weeks, nineteen staff, the overtime figure, and let the reader do the arithmetic. Every adjective in that document is a place where somebody decided what we were permitted to feel about a number.
Chair
We'll have to leave it there.
