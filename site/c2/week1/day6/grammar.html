The formal and formulaic subjunctive

صيغة الشرط الرسمية والمتجمّدة

🔎 In short / بإيجاز

Mandative subjunctive after verbs/adjectives of demand and fixed subjunctive expressions: I insist that he be present; It is essential that it remain; be that as it may; suffice it to say.

صيغة الشرط الرسمية بعد أفعال/صفات الطلب، والعبارات الشرطية الثابتة: I insist that he be present؛ be that as it may؛ suffice it to say.

🧩 The Pattern / النمط

verb of demand + that + BARE base verb (insist that he be) · were-subjunctive (if I were, as it were) · lest + bare verb · optative may (May it never happen) · frozen formulae (So be it, Be that as it may, Suffice it to say)

[I insist] [that he] [be] [informed]

⚡ After demand/recommend/request/insist + that, use the BARE base verb for every subject. 'Were' for all persons in hypotheticals. Never alter the frozen formulae.

بعد أفعال الطلب + that استخدم الفعل المجرّد لكل الفاعلين. و'Were' لكل الأشخاص في الافتراض. ولا تُغيّر التعبيرات المتجمّدة.

📍 When to use it / امتى تستخدمه

For formal demands, recommendations, motions and declarations. After insist, demand, request, propose, recommend, require and their nouns, the following verb is a bare base form for every subject, with no -s and no auxiliary. Also used in fixed expressions and in wishes. This is the register of committees, contracts, legal drafting and ceremonial speech.

للطلبات والتوصيات والمقترحات والإعلانات الرسمية. بعد أفعال مثل insist وdemand وrequest وpropose يأتي الفعل مجرّدًا لكل الفاعلين بلا -s وبلا مساعد. ويُستخدم أيضًا في التعبيرات الثابتة والتمنّي.

⚠️ Why this is tricky for Arabic speakers / ليه دي صعبة على العربي

Arabic marks its subjunctive-like mood morphologically and consistently (المنصوب), so learners instinctively supply agreement that English deliberately removes, producing 'that he is informed' or 'that she attends'. A second issue is that British English often prefers 'should' ('that he should be informed'), which is also correct, so learners meet two competing patterns and conclude the bare form is an error. The frozen formulae add a third problem: they cannot be altered at all.

💡 Examples / أمثلة

I insist that he be informed immediately.

أُصرّ على أن يُبلَّغ فورًا

[insist] + [that] + [bare base verb]

The committee recommended that she attend in person.

أوصت اللجنة بأن تحضر شخصيًا

[recommend] + [that] + [bare base verb]

We request that no exception be made.

نطلب ألّا يُستثنى أحد

[request] + [that] + [bare passive]

If I were you, I would wait.

لو كنت مكانك لانتظرت

[were] + subject (all persons)

Let me be clear, lest there be any doubt.

دعني أوضّح، خشية أن يكون هناك شكّ

[lest] + [bare base verb]

🚫 Common mistakes / أخطاء شائعة

❌ I insist that he is informed.

✅ I insist that he be informed.

After a verb of demand the following verb is a bare base form, with no agreement.

❌ The committee recommended that she attends.

✅ The committee recommended that she attend.

No -s on a subjunctive verb, even in the third person singular.

❌ If I was you, I would wait.

✅ If I were you, I would wait.

The were-subjunctive is used for every subject in hypothetical clauses.

❌ Lest there is any doubt.

✅ Lest there be any doubt.

'Lest' is followed by a bare base form.

🧠 Remember it / افتكرها

Demand strips the verb bare.

🎙️ Use it when you speak / استخدمها وانت بتتكلم

In accent week 1 you drilled the bare verb with a deliberate beat and a measured ceremonial pace. Because the verb carries no tense marker, the steadiness of your delivery is what makes the form sound intentional rather than mistaken.
