📄 Transcript / النص

Now read while you listen again — check what you missed. / اقرا وإنت بتسمع تاني — شوف إيه اللي فاتك.

Chair
We've time for two questions. Dr Mansour, you've had your hand up since the third slide.
Dr Mansour
Thank you. It's a nice paper and I want to be clear that my problem isn't with the estimate, it's with what the estimate is being asked to carry. You regress outcomes on the reform indicator, you include region and year fixed effects, and you report a coefficient of nought-point-one-nine. Fine. But the identifying assumption is that, absent the reform, treated and untreated districts would have moved in parallel. And your own figure two shows them diverging from 2011 — two years before anybody legislated. So the residual is doing the work, and I'd want to know what's in it.
Presenter
That's the right question and it's the one I'd have asked. Two responses. The pre-trend in figure two is driven almost entirely by four districts, all four of which are in the same governorate, and all four of which had a separate capital programme running from 2010. If I drop that governorate the pre-trend is flat to within noise — I have that as appendix table nine, and the coefficient moves from nought-point-one-nine to nought-point-one-seven, so it survives. What I'd concede is that I can't distinguish 'the pre-trend was an artefact of that programme' from 'the reform was targeted at districts already improving'. Those are observationally equivalent in my data and I should have said so in the paper rather than in answer to a question.
Dr Mansour
And with the governorate dropped, what happens to your standard errors?
Presenter
They widen. Substantially. Clustered at governorate level I'm down to eleven clusters, which is below where I'd want to be for the wild bootstrap to be reliable, so the honest statement is that the point estimate is stable and the inference is weak. I'd rather say that than report a t-statistic I don't believe.
Chair
Last question — at the back.
Questioner
A smaller one. Your outcome is administrative attainment data, and the reform changed who reports it. Is there a mechanical component?
Presenter
There is, and I've bounded it rather than removed it. The reform moved reporting from schools to districts in 2014, which is one year into treatment. If I restrict to the 2013 cohort only — reported under the old regime throughout — I get nought-point-one-four on a third of the sample. So roughly a quarter of the headline effect could be reporting rather than attainment. I put that in the abstract in the current draft, which is not where it was in the version you were sent.
Dr Mansour
That's a considerably better paper than the one I came to complain about.
Chair
On which note. Thank you both, and thank you all.
