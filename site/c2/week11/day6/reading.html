A Regime of Thrift

نظام من التوفير

نص عن الكلمات المحمّلة: نفس السلوك يوصف بالمدح أو التحقير حسب اختيار الكلمة.

📄 Read / اقرا

The new director is frugal.

Her predecessor was stingy.

As far as anyone can establish, they operate identical expenses policies.

This is not hypocrisy; it is vocabulary, and it is worth being precise about how it works.

Frugal and stingy denote the same behaviour and connote opposite evaluations — the first is complimentary, the second pejorative, and a writer choosing between them has already delivered a verdict while appearing to report.

Thrifty sits between, faintly approving and faintly dated.

The pairs are everywhere once noticed.

Assertive and pushy.

Slim and skinny.

Childlike and childish, where the same directness is extolled in one and belittled in the other.

A regime, incidentally, is what other people's arrangements are called; ours are policies.

What makes this hard to police is that neither word is false.

Nobody can be corrected for writing 'stingy' the way they can for writing a wrong date.

The slant is carried in the overtone, which is deniable by construction, and a writer accused of bias can retreat to the denotation with perfect honesty: I merely said she does not spend much.

The euphemism and its opposite are the same instrument pointed in different directions.

To laud is to insinuate merit; to denigrate is to insinuate its absence; and the neutral term, where one exists, is usually clumsy, which is why it loses.

'She spends less than the budget permits' is accurate, damning of nobody, and nine words long.

A modest proposal, then, for anyone drafting a profile.

Choose the flattering word or the damning one, but notice that you have chosen.

The one thing not available is the belief that you have simply described her.

What is the writer's main point?

The two directors have different expenses policies
Loaded words deliver a verdict while appearing to report
Pejorative words should be banned from profiles

❓ Question / سؤال 2

What is the joke in 'A regime, incidentally, is what other people's arrangements are called; ours are policies'?

إيه الطرفة في 'النظام هو اللي بنسمّي بيه ترتيبات غيرنا؛ وبتاعنا سياسات'؟

Regimes are always authoritarian
The same structure is labelled sinister or legitimate depending on whose it is
Policies are more carefully drafted than regimes

Why is the slant hard to police?

It is carried in the overtone, so the writer can retreat to the denotation honestly
Editors rarely read profiles
There are no neutral terms available
