🎯 The main point / الفكرة الأساسية

Answer this from listening. The transcript unlocks afterwards. / جاوب من السمع. النص بيتفتح بعد كده.

What finally stops the spokesman deflecting?

The minister arrives to answer in person
He suddenly admits the minister's statement to the House was false and offers to withdraw it on the spot
The chair of the committee intervenes
A reporter reads out sworn committee testimony that the register went into the box on the ninth

❓ Question / سؤال 1

What does the spokesman concede at the end?

That the minister deliberately misled the House in her formal statement
That the risk register was never produced
That the permanent secretary was mistaken
That he has no form of words, and will answer in writing today

❓ Question / سؤال 2

Which single word does Reporter 2 seize on, and why?

'Operational' — because it implies the document had no risk content
'Routinely' — because it leaves open what happened on this occasion
'Material' — because it narrows what counts as a concern
'Podium' — because it suggests he will answer elsewhere

❓ Question / سؤال 3

What does the spokesman say about who would issue any correction?

The minister herself, not him
The permanent secretary, as the sworn witness
The department's press office, later today
The committee, in its published report

❓ Question / سؤال 4

What alternative does Reporter 3 offer alongside 'she was made aware'?

That the transcript of Tuesday is inaccurate
That the register concerned a different programme
That the box was never opened, which is a different problem
That officials withheld the register after it was submitted

📄 Transcript / النص

Now read while you listen again — check what you missed. / اقرا وإنت بتسمع تاني — شوف إيه اللي فاتك.

Spokesman
I'll take three more and then I genuinely do have to go. Yes — at the front.
Reporter 1
Two questions. Did the minister see the risk register before the twelfth, and if she did, why does her statement to the House say she was 'not made aware of material concerns'?
Spokesman
On the second part, the minister's statement to the House stands, and I'd refer you to it in full rather than the phrase. On the first part, the risk register is an operational document. It's produced by officials, it's reviewed by officials, and it isn't routinely submitted to ministers.
Reporter 1
That isn't what I asked.
Spokesman
It's what I'm able to tell you. Yes, Layla—
Reporter 2
You said 'not routinely'. Was it submitted on this occasion?
Spokesman
I'm not going to get drawn into a document-by-document commentary from a podium.
Reporter 2
It's one document and it's the only one anybody's asking about—
Reporter 3
—and can I come in on that, because the permanent secretary told the committee on Tuesday that it was submitted—
Spokesman
I haven't seen the transcript of Tuesday, so I'm not going to characterise what the permanent secretary said or didn't say.
Reporter 3
I have it here. Question one-forty-one. Would you like me to read it?
Spokesman
...Go on.
Reporter 3
'It went in the box on the ninth.' That's the permanent secretary, under oath, about this register. So either the minister's box was not opened, which is a different problem, or she was made aware. Which is it?
Spokesman
...Right. I'm not going to stand here and pretend I have a form of words for that, because I don't, and inventing one now would be worse than saying so. I will take that away and we will come back to you today, in writing, on whether the register was in the box and whether it was read. I'd ask you not to read anything into the fact that I can't answer it standing here, except that I can't answer it standing here.
Reporter 1
Will the minister correct the record?
Spokesman
If the answer to what I've just undertaken to find out requires a correction, then a correction will follow, and it will follow from her and not from me. That's the last one. Thank you.
Reporter 2
Today, you said. On the record — today.
Spokesman
Today. On the record. Thank you.
