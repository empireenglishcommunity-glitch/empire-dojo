Not Bad, Considering

مش وحش، بالنسبة

نص عن التقليل والسخرية الجافة: 'مش وحش' ممكن تكون أعلى مدح أو أقسى نقد.

📄 Read / اقرا

'Not bad at all,' said my supervisor, of a thesis chapter that had taken eleven weeks.

I spent a further two working out what he meant, which was, I now believe, that it was good.

Litotes is the figure in which affirmation is achieved by denying the contrary, and English is unusually committed to it.

'No small feat' means considerable.

'Not exactly a triumph' means a disaster.

'Somewhat concerned' means alarmed, and 'I'm not entirely convinced' has ended more projects than any sentence containing the word 'no'.

What makes it treacherous is not the construction but the delivery.

The same four words, deadpan, can be the highest praise available to a certain kind of person, or the driest possible dismissal, and the only thing distinguishing them is an intonation that no transcript preserves.

This is why understatement travels badly and why it is the last thing a very advanced learner acquires: every component is simple and the meaning is carried entirely outside them.

It is also, one has to concede, sometimes a coward's instrument.

'Hardly ideal' permits a manager to register serious criticism while retaining the option of having meant it mildly, and if challenged he may deflate the whole thing — banter, surely — leaving the recipient to look sardonic for having noticed.

Irony is double-edged in precisely that way: it insinuates while preserving deniability, and the deniability is the appeal.

'It's just a scratch,' says the man with the severed arm, and we laugh because the gap between the words and the world is doing the work.

Wry, mock-modest, facetious: the family is large, and its members are not interchangeable.

Getting one wrong is not a small error either, though I would not call it a catastrophe.

it a catastrophe.

What does he concede about 'hardly ideal'?
