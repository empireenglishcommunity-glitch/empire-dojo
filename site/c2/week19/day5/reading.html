Or Rather, What I Meant Was

أو بالأحرى، اللي كنت أقصده

نص عن الطلاقة الشفهية مكتوب بنفس تعثّراتها، والفكرة إن التصحيح الذاتي مهارة.

📄 Read / اقرا

The thing is — and I want to put this carefully, or rather I want to put it twice, because the first version will be wrong — spontaneous fluency is not the absence of hesitation.

That is to say, it is the presence of repair.

Let me rephrase.

What I'm getting at is that a fluent speaker and a rapid speaker are different animals, and we assess the wrong one.

Listen to somebody genuinely articulate and you will hear circumlocution, self-correction, backtracking, the odd filler, and a great many phrases whose only function is to hold the floor while the sentence is assembled — 'the thing is', 'as it were', 'how shall I put it'.

Transcribed, it is a mess.

Heard, it is effortless, and the effortlessness is what the repair buys.

Whereas — and this is the part I get wrong when I teach it — the speaker with no hesitation at all is often not fluent but rehearsed, and you find this out the moment the topic moves.

Off the cuff, fluency is visible precisely in the recovery: a paraphrase produced mid-clause, a reformulation offered before the listener has noticed anything was needed.

I should be honest that this is easy to overstate.

Hesitation is not self-evidently skill; a learner who stops every four words is not demonstrating poise, and pretending otherwise would be a kindness that costs them the exam.

The distinction is whether the pause is doing work.

So, to put it differently, and then I will stop: seamless is not the same as unforced.

Seamless can be memorised.

Unforced cannot, and the tell is that an unforced speaker will occasionally say 'no, that's not quite it' — which sounds like a failure and is in fact the only unfakeable evidence of the whole business.

he whole business.
