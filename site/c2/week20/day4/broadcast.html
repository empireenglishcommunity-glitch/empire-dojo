👂 Before you listen / قبل ما تسمع

🎯 The main point / الفكرة الأساسية

What does the review conclude the real problem was?

Ehab's seven-minute delay in escalating the alarm
A contradiction between the alarm procedure and the maintenance schedule, plus a mis-triaged sensor
Nadia's decision to sign off the procedure in the spring
The failure of the west loop sensor itself

📄 Transcript / النص

Now read while you listen again — check what you missed. / اقرا وإنت بتسمع تاني — شوف إيه اللي فاتك.

Facilitator
Ground rules, quickly, because we've done this badly before. This is not a disciplinary. Nothing said here goes in anybody's file. I will write one page and you will all see it before anybody else does. Ehab, you had the desk — start at the alarm.
Ehab
Alarm came in at eleven fifty-one as a low-level pressure warning on the west loop. I've had four of those this month, all four were the sensor, so I did what I've done four times: acknowledged it, logged it, and carried on with the changeover. At eleven fifty-eight it escalated and I called it in. So seven minutes. And I want to say the seven minutes out loud before somebody else does, because it's the number this whole thing is about.
Nadia
Can I stop you there. It isn't the number this is about. Ehab, four out of four of those alarms were false, and the procedure you followed is the procedure we wrote. If seven minutes is the finding then the finding is about me, because I signed the procedure off in the spring and I signed it off knowing the sensor on the west loop was unreliable.
Fouad
With respect, that's generous but it's also a bit convenient. The procedure says acknowledge and log. It doesn't say carry on with a changeover. There's a line in it about not undertaking non-essential work with an unresolved alarm.
Ehab
The changeover isn't non-essential, it's on the schedule.
Fouad
I know, and that's my point, not a criticism of you. We've got a procedure that forbids non-essential work and a schedule that makes everything essential. Those two documents have never been read next to each other.
Facilitator
Say that again slowly, because I think that's the finding.
Fouad
Any operator on that desk at eleven fifty-one, following both documents exactly, does what Ehab did. Which means Ehab is not the variable. If you put me on that desk, or Nadia, we get the same seven minutes, and a review that ends with 'operator response time' has told us the one thing we already knew.
Nadia
And the sensor is still in. That's the other thing. It's been on the list since March as low priority, and 'low priority' is a judgement somebody made when the failure mode looked like nuisance alarms rather than seven minutes.
Facilitator
Right. So what I'm hearing is three findings and none of them is about Ehab. One: the alarm procedure and the maintenance schedule contradict each other. Two: the west loop sensor was triaged on a failure mode we now know was wrong. Three — and this one's mine to fix — we ran the last two reviews looking for the person, so the honest answer is that people have stopped bringing us the near misses. Ehab, is that fair to you?
Ehab
It's fairer than I expected walking in. I'd add a fourth, and I'd rather it came from me. I didn't ring Nadia at eleven fifty-two. I could have. It's a thirty-second call and I've got her number, and I didn't make it because I didn't want to be the man who rings about a sensor for the fifth time. That's not the procedure. That's me. And if the page doesn't say that, the page is missing something.
Facilitator
...Then the page will say it. Thank you.
