On the Face of It

في الظاهر

نص عن درجات اليقين: كل عبارة تحوّط بتحدّد مصدر المعرفة، والفروق بينها كبيرة.

📄 Read / اقرا

By all accounts the valve was closed.

It stands to reason that it was: the procedure requires it, the operator is experienced, and to the best of my knowledge no one has suggested otherwise.

On the face of it, then, the matter is settled.

It is not, and the reason is that every sentence in that paragraph reports a different kind of warrant.

'By all accounts' is testimony.

'It stands to reason' is inference from the general to the particular, which is the weakest of the three and the most persuasive.

'To the best of my knowledge' is an absence of contrary evidence, which is not evidence.

Presumably the reader aggregates them into a single impression of likelihood, and in all likelihood that impression is stronger than any of the components warrants.

If anything, the epistemic vocabulary of English is too rich here rather than too poor.

Purportedly, ostensibly, seemingly, evidently, demonstrably — the last of these is a claim about proof and the other four are claims about appearance, and they arrive in the same syntactic slot with the same unremarkable rhythm.

As far as I can tell, no reader distinguishes them reliably at speed, and I include myself.

What is tenable, then?

Only this: that a conjecture may be plausible, that a surmise may be warranted, and that neither is verifiable until somebody has attested to something specific.

To my mind the test is procedural rather than logical.

Postulate the opposite and ask what would have to be found.

If the answer is 'a record', the claim is speculative until the record is produced.

All the evidence suggests we are poor at this.

There is reason to believe we are poorest precisely where the stakes make care most necessary, which is where the vocabulary is thickest.

❓ Question / سؤال 1

What is the writer's central point about the opening paragraph?

Each sentence reports a different kind of warrant, which readers aggregate wrongly
The valve was probably not closed
Testimony is always stronger than inference

❓ Question / سؤال 2

Which does he call the weakest and most persuasive?

'By all accounts' — testimony
'It stands to reason' — inference from the general to the particular
'Demonstrably' — a claim about proof

❓ Question / سؤال 3

What procedural test does he propose?

Count the hedges in each paragraph and reject any claim that carries too many
Require testimony from two independent sources
Postulate the opposite and ask what would have to be found
