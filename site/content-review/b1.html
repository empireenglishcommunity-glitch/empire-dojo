What Happens If We Waste Water?

إيه اللي يحصل لو ضيّعنا الميّة؟
B1.R.4 B1.R.5

165 words

Water looks free, but it is not. If a city wastes water today, it will pay for it tomorrow.

Consider one simple example. If a tap leaks one drop every second, it will waste about thirty litres in a day. That is one family. Now imagine a thousand families in the same area. Unless somebody repairs those taps, the result is a serious loss of clean water.

There is a second consequence. As soon as clean water becomes rare, its price rises, and poorer families are affected first. In case you think this is a distant problem, remember that several large cities have already reduced supply for a few hours a day.

The good news is that small actions help. If we repair leaks, reuse water for plants, and recycle where possible, we reduce waste immediately.

The main conclusion is clear: we will not solve this problem by waiting for governments. It will be solved when ordinary families decide to protect what they already have.

Every office has its own small mystery. In ours, the store-room keys went missing on Monday morning.

We had three pieces of evidence. First, the keys can't be inside the store room, because the door was locked from the outside — that is impossible. Second, only four people have permission to take them, so it is certain that one of us moved them. Third, the camera shows that nobody entered the corridor after six on Sunday.

On Wednesday a colleague called and explained everything: the keys were in his jacket. He had put them there and forgotten. No detective was needed. The reason was ordinary, and that is usually the right conclusion — the simplest explanation solves most mysteries.

Is It Worth the Money?

هل يستاهل الفلوس؟
B1.R.5

170 words

Most of us do not waste money on big things. We waste it on small things we do not notice.

Think about a cheap phone charger. It costs too little to worry about, so we buy it, and it breaks in a month. Then we buy another one. After a year we have spent plenty of money and we still do not have a charger that works. A better one would have been expensive enough to make us think — and cheap enough in the end.

Of course, the opposite mistake exists too. Some people believe that a high price always means good value, so they spend far too much and call it an investment. Neither habit is wise.

The conclusion is not 'buy cheap' or 'buy expensive'. It is: buy rarely, and buy once.
